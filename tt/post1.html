A while back I clicked a link on my own site and landed on a 404 page. It had been broken for weeks. Nobody told me, and I had no idea how many other links were dead too.

That's the thing about broken links. They don't crash your site. They just quietly annoy visitors and make Google trust your pages a little less. The good news is that finding them takes about a minute, and it doesn't cost anything.

Here's exactly how I check mine.

What counts as a broken link?

A broken link is any link that doesn't lead where it should. Most of the time that means one of these:

  • 404 Not Found: the page was deleted or its address changed.
  • 410 Gone: the page was removed on purpose.
  • 5xx errors: the other site's server is down or struggling.
  • Timeouts: the page takes so long that the browser gives up.

Redirects (301 and 308) aren't broken, but too many of them slow things down. If a link redirects, it's usually worth pointing it straight at the final address.

Why it's worth fixing

Three simple reasons:

  1. Visitors leave. Hit a dead link and most people don't hunt around. They hit back.
  2. Google wastes time. Every dead link is a dead end for the crawler that's trying to discover your pages.
  3. It looks abandoned. A site full of broken links feels unmaintained, even when it isn't.

How often should you check?

Once a month is plenty for most small sites. If you publish every day or link out to a lot of other websites, every week or two is better. I just put a reminder on my phone for the first of the month.
