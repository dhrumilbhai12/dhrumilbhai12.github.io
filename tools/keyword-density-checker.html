What this keyword density checker does

Keyword density is a descriptive measure: phrase occurrences divided by the total word count. It can help editors notice repeated wording or a topic that barely appears, but it is not a ranking formula. Modern search systems interpret meaning in context. A useful page answers the reader’s question clearly, uses natural terminology and avoids awkward repetition.

How to use this tool

  1. Read the fields and enter or paste the information you want to review.
  2. Adjust the available options to match the page, text or crawler setup you are working on.
  3. Review the generated counts, preview or report. Check any warning against your actual goal.
  4. Copy, download or apply the result, then verify it on the destination page or in your normal workflow.

Practical tips

  • Check a full section or article rather than a single sentence for a more stable picture.
  • Use the stop word toggle to focus on meaningful terms, then turn it off to inspect exact text patterns.
  • Review two and three word phrases because they often capture a topic more clearly than isolated words.
  • Treat percentages as an editing aid, not a target to hit.
  • Read every suggested phrase in context before changing your draft; some repetition is useful for clarity.

Interpreting your results

The report intentionally limits its focus to short contiguous phrases. It does not understand synonyms, entities or whether a sentence is accurate. That makes it useful as a quick review, not a substitute for reading. A phrase appearing often may be a navigation label or an essential product name rather than over-optimization. Conversely, a page can answer a topic well without repeating the exact same phrase. Use the counts to locate passages worth rereading, then decide whether a clearer example, heading or synonym would help. The best edit is the one that makes the page easier to understand while preserving the intended meaning.

Frequently asked questions

How is keyword density calculated?

Density is phrase count divided by the total word count, multiplied by 100. The table shows both count and percentage.

Are stop words removed?

You choose. With the toggle on, common connecting words are omitted from phrase results. They are still part of the total word count.

Does high keyword density improve rankings?

No. Repeating phrases unnaturally can make writing less useful. Use the report to find patterns and edit for readers.

Why check two and three word phrases?

Multiword phrases reveal specific topics and wording that a one-word list can hide.

Is my text uploaded?

No. Analysis runs locally in your browser; the pasted text is not sent to a server.
