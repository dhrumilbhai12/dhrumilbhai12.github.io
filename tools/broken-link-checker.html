What this broken link checker does

A broken link frustrates visitors and can interrupt a useful journey through a site. Regular checks are especially helpful after a migration, redesign or content cleanup. Status codes are clues, not the whole story: 301 and 302 responses usually indicate a redirect, 404 means the destination was not found, and 403 may indicate access restrictions or bot blocking. Some sites reject automated checks or require a browser session, so verify an error manually before removing a link.

How to use this tool

  1. Read the fields and enter or paste the information you want to review.
  2. Adjust the available options to match the page, text or crawler setup you are working on.
  3. Review the generated counts, preview or report. Check any warning against your actual goal.
  4. Copy, download or apply the result, then verify it on the destination page or in your normal workflow.

Practical tips

  • Start with a public page that does not require a login.
  • Run checks after changing a domain, moving content or removing old campaign pages.
  • Review redirects and update internal links to the final destination when practical.
  • Treat timeouts and access-denied responses as uncertain; open the destination in a normal browser before deciding it is broken.
  • Use the CSV export to share a cleanup list with a site owner or content editor.

For important site changes, verify the final result in context and retain a copy of any original content you replace.

Interpreting your results

A scan is a snapshot, and a remote website may behave differently from one request to the next. Rate limits, temporary outages, bot defenses, geo restrictions and login walls can all affect a response. The checker caps the number of links to keep a page scan manageable; large sites should be checked in sections or with a dedicated site crawl. When a destination is important, open it directly, follow any redirect and confirm that it still provides the expected information. Then update links at their source so visitors land on the most useful current page.

Frequently asked questions

What does the checker inspect?

It reads links from the supplied page and checks up to 100 unique HTTP or HTTPS destinations.

Why can a working link show an error?

Some sites block automated requests, need cookies or respond slowly. Confirm errors in a regular browser before acting.

What do redirect results mean?

The destination responds by sending the request to another URL. The result includes the final URL when available.

How many links are checked?

The worker checks at most 100 unique links from one page to keep each scan bounded.

Can I check a private page?

No. The page must be publicly reachable by the checker without login or private network access.
