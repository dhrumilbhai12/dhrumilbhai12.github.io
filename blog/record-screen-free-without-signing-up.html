How to record your screen for free without signing up (2026)

Tinkro team · 2 November 2026 · 3 min read

You need to record your screen — a bug for a developer, a tutorial for a colleague, a walkthrough for a client — and the first three tools you find all want an email address, a password and a credit card “for the trial”. You don't need any of that. Every major operating system now ships with a screen recorder, and modern browsers can record your screen without installing anything at all.

Here's every genuinely free, no-signup option in 2026, and when to use each one.

1. In your browser (any desktop OS)

Modern browsers include two features that make account-free screen recording possible: getDisplayMedia, which lets a page ask to capture your screen, window or tab, and MediaRecorder, which turns that stream into a video file. A browser-based recorder like the Tinkro free screen recorder uses both, so the video is encoded on your own computer and downloaded straight to it.

  1. Open the recorder in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari 16+.
  2. Choose microphone, camera bubble and audio options.
  3. Click Start recording and pick a screen, window or tab.
  4. Stop, preview and download as WebM (or MP4 where your browser supports it).

Best for: quick recordings on a work computer where you can't install software, and anyone who wants their face in a bubble without extra apps. Limits: it doesn't work on phones, and system audio capture depends on your browser and OS — Chrome and Edge can capture tab audio, and system audio on Windows.

2. Windows 11: Snipping Tool

The Snipping Tool in Windows 11 can record video, not just screenshots. Open it (press Win + Shift + S and switch to the video icon, or open Snipping Tool from Start), choose Record, drag a rectangle over the area you want, and press Start. Recent versions can include microphone and system audio. Recordings are saved as MP4.

Windows also has the Xbox Game Bar (Win + G), which records a single app window. It's designed for games and can't record the desktop or File Explorer, so the Snipping Tool is usually the better choice for work.

3. macOS: Screenshot toolbar

Press Cmd + Shift + 5 to open the Screenshot toolbar. Choose “Record Entire Screen” or “Record Selected Portion”, then open Options to pick a microphone and a save location. Click Record, and stop with the button in the menu bar. You get a .mov file. QuickTime Player (File → New Screen Recording) does the same thing.

macOS doesn't record system audio out of the box. If you need sound from your Mac itself, a browser-based recorder capturing a Chrome tab with audio is the simplest workaround.

4. ChromeOS: Screen capture

On a Chromebook, open Quick Settings and choose Screen capture, or press Ctrl + Shift + Show windows. Switch to the video camera icon, choose full screen, a window or a partial area, and start recording. You can turn on the microphone and, on recent versions, show your camera.

5. iPhone and iPad

Add Screen Recording to Control Centre (Settings → Control Centre), then open Control Centre and tap the record button. Long-press it to turn the microphone on. Recordings go to Photos. Note that web pages can't record your screen on iOS, so browser-based recorders won't work there.

6. Android

Most phones running Android 11 or later have Screen record in Quick Settings (swipe down twice; you may need to edit the tiles to add it). Choose whether to record audio and whether to show touches, then start. Names and options vary slightly between manufacturers.

7. OBS Studio (free, open source)

If you record often or need full control — multiple sources, scenes, overlays, high bitrates, streaming — OBS Studio is free, open source and needs no account. It runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. The trade-off is a learning curve: expect to spend a few minutes setting up a scene with a Display Capture source and your microphone before your first recording.

Which one should you use?

  • One-off recording at work, nothing to install: a browser recorder.
  • You're on Windows 11 and want MP4: Snipping Tool.
  • You're on a Mac: Cmd + Shift + 5.
  • On a phone: the built-in recorder in Control Centre / Quick Settings.
  • Regular, polished recordings: OBS Studio.

Tips for better recordings

  • Record a window, not the whole screen, so notifications and private tabs stay out of the video.
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb before you start.
  • Plan three points, say them, and stop. Short recordings get watched.
  • Use a headset if you capture system audio and talk at the same time, to avoid echo.
  • Check the file size before emailing: a few minutes of HD video can be tens of megabytes. Upload it to a drive and share a link instead.

What about privacy?

Built-in OS tools and browser-based recorders that run locally keep your video on your device. Cloud recorders upload your video to their servers so they can give you a link, which is convenient but means your recording lives on someone else's infrastructure. If you're recording anything sensitive, prefer a local tool and share the file deliberately.
